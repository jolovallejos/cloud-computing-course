## 2. What similarities exist among AWS, Azure, and GCP?

Despite their differences in market position and specialization, all three cloud platforms share fundamental similarities in their service offerings and architectural approaches. First, they all provide core Infrastructure-as-a-Service (IaaS) capabilities including virtual machines, object storage, and virtual networking with similar underlying concepts and features.

Second, each platform offers comparable Platform-as-a-Service (PaaS) services for databases, serverless computing, and container orchestration. The service categories are remarkably similar across all three providers, with direct equivalents for each major service type:
- Virtual Machines: EC2 / Azure VMs / Compute Engine
- Object Storage: S3 / Blob Storage / Cloud Storage
- SQL Databases: RDS / SQL Database / Cloud SQL
- Kubernetes: EKS / AKS / GKE

Third, all three providers offer identity and access management (IAM), cost management tools, monitoring and logging services, and global infrastructure with multiple regions and availability zones. Each platform provides comprehensive APIs, SDKs, and cloud management consoles with similar user experiences focused on resource provisioning, monitoring, and administration.

## 3. How do business requirements influence cloud platform selection?

Business requirements are paramount in cloud platform selection and can dramatically influence the final decision. Organizations must carefully consider their specific needs, existing technology investments, and strategic goals when choosing a cloud provider. There is no "one-size-fits-all" solution, and the best platform depends entirely on the organization's unique circumstances.

Existing technology investments play a crucial role in the decision-making process. For example, an organization already using Windows Server, Active Directory, Office 365, and other Microsoft technologies would benefit significantly from Microsoft Azure's native integration capabilities. This integration reduces migration complexity, lowers operational overhead, and leverages existing staff expertise, making the transition smoother and more cost-effective.

Cost structure and pricing models are also critical factors that influence platform selection. Budget-conscious startups with limited capital may prefer GCP's generous free credits and simple pricing models, making it easier to get started without significant upfront investment. In contrast, enterprise organizations with established budgets might prioritize AWS's mature enterprise features, extensive partner ecosystem, and proven reliability at scale. Technical requirements like high-performance AI/ML training naturally lead toward GCP's specialized TPU hardware and Vertex AI platform.

## 4. What did you learn from acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant?

Acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant taught me that technical decisions must always align with business objectives and requirements. I learned to analyze customer scenarios holistically—understanding their existing infrastructure, specific needs, budget constraints, and growth projections before making any recommendation. This comprehensive approach ensures that the proposed solution truly addresses the client's challenges.

The role requires balancing technical capabilities with practical considerations like cost, learning curve, and operational complexity. I discovered that effective solution architects don't simply choose a platform because it's technically "best" but because it best solves the client's specific challenges and aligns with their strategic goals. Sometimes the technically superior option may not be the right choice due to budget constraints or skillset limitations.

I also learned the importance of understanding each platform's unique strengths and weaknesses to make informed recommendations. AWS excels in service breadth and market maturity, making it a safe choice for enterprises. Azure dominates in Microsoft integration and hybrid cloud scenarios. GCP leads in AI/ML innovation, data analytics, and Kubernetes expertise. This multi-cloud perspective is essential for providing valuable, objective guidance to clients.
